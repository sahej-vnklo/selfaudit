## SECTION 2: UNDER THE HOOD
*How SelfAudit actually works — the engineering blueprint.*

---

### The Core Philosophy

The voice is the interface. The ontology is the engine.

SelfAudit is local-first. Your business model — your units, your definitions, your historical data — lives on your machine, not a shared cloud server. This is a deliberate architectural choice. It means your operational blueprint is private, fast, and yours. The AI intelligence layer processes your data in context but your schema never leaves your environment without your permission.

The call works because the model is always current. Integration data syncs continuously. Snapshots are taken on schedule. By the time you dial in, everything is already processed and ready to surface.

---

### The File System as the Ontology

When a user is configured, the system writes a structured folder hierarchy to their local machine. This is the ontology — the live model of their business.

```
~/.selfaudit/
  config.json               ← industry, global settings
  units/
    customer-service/
      schema.json           ← unit definition, properties, targets, context
      integrations.json     ← which tools are connected to this unit
      objects/
        agent-john-doe.json ← individual entities with properties
        agent-jane-smith.json
      snapshots/
        2026-06-09.json     ← historical state captures
    sales/
      schema.json
      objects/
      snapshots/
    finance/
      ...
```

Every unit is a folder. Every defined entity inside that unit is a JSON file. Every schema file describes the unit's structure — properties, targets, relationships.

This is the business ontology. Human-readable, version-controllable, entirely owned by the user.

---

### Schema Files — What They Contain

A unit schema is the semantic definition of that part of the business:

- **Unit name and description** — plain English
- **Properties** — the dimensions that matter (headcount, response time target, ticket volume threshold)
- **Object types** — what individual entities exist inside this unit (employees, accounts, products)
- **Relationships** — how this unit connects to others (Customer Service affects Retention, which lives in Revenue)
- **Integration mappings** — which fields from Zoho/HubSpot/etc. map to which properties
- **Baseline targets** — what "healthy" looks like, defined by the user

---

### The Integration Layer

SelfAudit connects to external tools via OAuth. Each integration runs a data adapter — a mapping layer that translates raw API responses into the schema language of the relevant unit.

Zoho CRM returns raw deal and contact data. The SelfAudit adapter maps deal stages → Sales pipeline properties, contact activity → Customer Service touchpoints, and writes the normalised output into local object files.

Delta syncs — only changes are pulled, not full data dumps. Local files are always the source of truth; integration data enriches them continuously.

---

### The Intelligence Loop

Runs per unit, on schedule and on trigger.

**Step 1 — Context assembly**
Load the unit schema (structure + targets), current object files (live state), latest integration data, and snapshot history (trend context).

**Step 2 — Prompt construction**
A structured prompt gives the AI full context: what this unit is, what healthy looks like, what the data shows, what changed since the last snapshot. Engineered to produce diagnosis, second-order effects, root cause, and next steps — not summary.

**Step 3 — AI analysis**
Claude processes the full context and returns structured intelligence: what's notable, what it connects to across units, what the downstream effect is, what the recommended action is.

**Step 4 — Snapshot and surface**
Output stored as a timestamped snapshot. Available immediately on the voice call, in the interface, and in the morning brief.

---

### Cross-Unit Intelligence

The most powerful layer. A Customer Service degradation + Sales velocity drop + churn uptick → not three separate alerts. One compound pattern, one root cause that spans all three.

This is second-order intelligence — the thing a sharp COO catches after three weeks of watching. SelfAudit catches it in real time, every time.

---

### Why Local-First Matters

- **Speed** — reads from local disk, not a remote database
- **Privacy** — the business model never lives on a shared server
- **Trust** — founders connect sensitive integrations because the data stays theirs
- **Resilience** — works without internet for read operations
- **Portability** — the entire ontology is a folder. Back it up, move it, version it

### THE HYBRID MECHANISM IN PLAIN ENGLISH
*Read this FIRST. Every answer below is built on this mechanism. If you memorize the answers without understanding this, a partner pokes one layer deeper and finds the floor. Understand it — then the answers below become things you're describing, not reciting.*

---

**One event, traced through both architectures. The event: a deal closes in HubSpot.**

**The cloud version (how the prototype works today):**

1. A deal closes in HubSpot.
2. HubSpot fires a webhook to our server.
3. Our server writes it into our database (Supabase Postgres, in the cloud).
4. On a schedule, a function reads the rows, builds a prompt, sends it to Claude.
5. Claude's diagnosis is written back to our database.
6. The user's browser reads from our database and displays it.

Everything — raw data, history, analysis — lives on our servers. Simple. Fast to build. It's how one non-technical founder shipped the whole loop solo. The cost is trust: a founder connecting QuickBooks is putting their full ledger in our database.

**The hybrid version (what the funded build migrates to). Same event:**

1. A deal closes in HubSpot.
2. HubSpot fires a webhook to a tiny relay server in the cloud. The relay encrypts the payload with the founder's public key and holds it in a queue. It cannot read it. It stores nothing.
3. A background app on the founder's laptop pulls the payload from the queue, decrypts it with a private key that exists only on that machine, and saves it as a JSON file in the `.selfaudit/` folder.
4. Once a day (and on trigger), that app reads all the local files and computes a short summary — *"sales velocity down 18%, 3 deals stalled at contract stage."* Plain code doing counting and averaging. No AI needed. All on the laptop.
5. The app sends only that summary — roughly 80 tokens, no names, no amounts — to Claude. Claude reasons across the summaries from every unit and returns the diagnosis.
6. The diagnosis is saved back to the laptop as a snapshot. When the founder calls the voice line, it reads from there — the analysis is already sitting on disk.

**The only differences, side by side:**

| Step | Cloud (today) | Hybrid (target) |
|---|---|---|
| Webhook lands at | our server | a relay that can't read it |
| Raw data stored in | our database | a folder on the founder's laptop |
| Summarizing done by | our cloud function | the app on their laptop |
| Claude reads | rows from our database | a short summary sent from the laptop |
| Answer stored in | our database | a file on the laptop |

Same pipeline. Same loop. Same Claude. The only thing that moves is *where the raw data sits* and *who does the grunt work*.

---

**THE TRAP TO NEVER FALL INTO: "so it's basically local, the cloud does nothing."**

Wrong — and if you say it, a partner will dismantle you. The cloud does two jobs, and one of them is the most important job in the system:

| | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Storage of raw data | Local | privacy, trust, cost |
| Parsing and summarizing | Local | cheap grunt work — counting, not thinking |
| **The thinking** | **Cloud (Claude)** | a frontier model physically cannot run on a laptop |
| The voice line | Cloud (Twilio/Vapi) | phone networks are cloud by definition |

The correct sentence: **the brain is in the cloud, the filing cabinet is on the laptop.** The intelligence — the cross-unit reasoning, the diagnosis, the second-order projection — happens on Anthropic's servers. It just happens on dense summaries instead of raw data. "The cloud never sees the ledger" does not mean the cloud does nothing. It means the cloud sees the shape of the problem, not the contents.

### The Local Sync Engine — How Local-First Gets Real-Time Data

**Partner's Question:** "If it's local-first, how are you running continuous background syncs via OAuth without a cloud server acting as a middleman?"

We use a hybrid architecture: a stateless cloud-bridge combined with a local background daemon.

The cloud-bridge is a simple, zero-data-retention worker (deployed via Cloudflare Workers). It holds our OAuth client credentials and acts as the public webhook listener for platforms like HubSpot, Zoho, and GitHub. When a webhook fires — a ticket closes, a deal moves — the worker receives the payload, encrypts it instantly using the user's public key, and pushes it into a lightweight message queue.

On the user's local machine, a native background daemon (written in Go or Rust for low memory footprint) maintains a persistent WebSocket connection to that queue. It pulls down the encrypted payloads, decrypts them locally using the private key, passes them through our local data adapters, and commits delta changes directly to the `.selfaudit/units/` directory.

No business data ever sits unencrypted on a cloud server.

### Moving From Recommendation to Agentic Execution

**Partner's Question:** "Agents can execute. Right now your system tells the founder what to do. How do you handle execution safely?"

We shifted the intelligence loop from a passive diagnostic prompt to an event-driven agentic loop. When an anomaly is detected — say, onboarding drop-off in the March cohort — the system doesn't just write a text summary.

It spawns an execution agent that uses tool-calling to generate concrete payloads. It calls our HubSpot adapter to draft a targeted campaign for that cohort, and drafts an alert payload for Slack. Instead of executing autonomously, it appends an `actions_pending/` array to the local snapshot file.

When the founder dials in, the AI says: *"I've drafted a remediation campaign in HubSpot to pause the flow and route that cohort manually. Press 1 to execute now."*

If they press 1, the local daemon pushes the pre-staged API calls out to the tools. The founder stays in control. Execution is staged, not autonomous.

### The Multi-Agent Reasoning Architecture

**Partner's Question:** "If you throw an entire company's context into one Claude prompt, you'll hit token limits or get hallucinations. How do you reason across the whole business?"

We don't use a monolithic prompt. We built a hierarchical multi-agent system with localised context windows.

**Level 1 — Unit Analysts:** Each unit has its own dedicated lightweight agent loop. The Customer Service agent only reads `units/customer-service/schema.json` and its immediate snapshots. It outputs a compact structured Anomaly & Trend vector — a JSON summary, not raw data.

**Level 2 — The Chief Operating Agent:** A master orchestrator takes only the compact JSON summaries from Level 1 agents, alongside a global metadata map of cross-unit relationships. It reasons at the correlation layer — not at the data layer.

This drastically reduces token overhead, isolates noise, and lets Claude focus entirely on higher-order patterns — like realising a Customer Service headcount drop is leading to a Sales pipeline decay 60 days out.

### Local Role-Based Access Control

**Partner's Question:** "If the founder puts this folder on a shared drive, what stops a regular employee from querying sensitive finance data through the voice interface?"

We implement security at the cryptographic and file-system level — not application-level logic.

Every user profile (Founder, COO, Manager) is assigned a cryptographic keypair. Sensitive directories — `units/finance/`, `units/hr/` — are encrypted locally using a symmetric key that's only decryptable by authorised user keys.

When a user initiates a voice session, the local daemon authenticates their local session token. If a Sales Manager calls in, the voice interface simply cannot read or decrypt the JSON files inside `finance/`. To the AI engine running that session, those files don't exist.

The security layer is architectural. There's no application-level permission table to misconfigure or bypass.

### Cross-Functional Reasoning — How Local Files and Cloud Actually Work Together

**Partner's Question:** "How does cross-functional reasoning across a local file system and a cloud server actually work mechanically?"

The core mechanic is Summary-Conditioned Hierarchical Inference. We never stream raw file data to the cloud. We separate the data parsing layer (local) from the global reasoning layer (cloud).

**Step 1 — Local State Consolidation**
The local background daemon watches `.selfaudit/units/`. Each unit is processed by a small, hyper-fast quantized local model — its only job is to turn raw transaction logs and tool outputs into dense, standardised Unit State Briefs: compact JSON chunks summarising trends, deltas, and anomalies for that unit.

**Step 2 — Abstracted Escalation**
When a cross-functional question comes in — *"How is our cash flow affecting onboarding response times?"* — the daemon collects State Briefs from Finance and Customer Service. It does not upload raw financial ledgers or private chat logs. It uploads only the high-density metadata abstracts.

**Step 3 — Cloud Frontier Orchestration**
The cloud tier (Claude) receives the compact, anonymised state abstracts alongside the global structural map of cross-unit dependencies. It reasons across the data points, matches operational timestamps, diagnoses the correlation, and returns a structured instruction payload.

**Step 4 — Local Execution**
The local daemon receives that payload and applies it — triggering local scripts, updating the UI, or preparing the voice stream for the founder's call.

Raw data never leaves the machine. The cloud never sees the ledger. It only sees the shape of the problem.

### Handling Volume at Scale — The Temporal Knowledge Graph

**Partner's Question:** "How do you take on a massive volume of data, handle it efficiently, and still surface second-order issues like a shipping delay causing churn three months later?"

Parsing millions of raw rows through an LLM on every query would crash the system or cost thousands of dollars per run. We don't do that. We use a Temporal Knowledge Graph Engine operating on an event-driven rolling delta pipeline.

**Data Reduction via Rolling Vector Accumulation**

The local daemon treats data as a stream, not a pile. When syncing with tool histories, it processes data once — chunk by chunk — extracts core semantic meaning, maps it to the graph, then archives or drops the raw noise. The system maintains a continuously updated lightweight snapshot index rather than re-reading everything from scratch.

**How Second-Order Correlation Actually Works**

Second-order issues are invisible to keyword search and basic RAG because causes and effects are separated by time and live in different applications. To catch them, the system builds a Dependency Graph:

- **Nodes:** Objects like `Developer: Alice`, `Repository: Auth-Module`, `Metric: Avg-Response-Time`, `Cohort: March-Users`
- **Edges:** Causal relationships with explicit timestamps

Example path the system builds automatically:

> **Event A (GitHub):** Code velocity on Auth-Module drops 40% — May 2nd
> **Event B (HubSpot):** Support tickets tagged "Login Error" spike — May 10th
> **Event C (Stripe):** March cohort churn climbs 12% — June 1st

The cloud reasoning layer doesn't read the actual code or individual emails. It reads the metadata structure of the graph path:

`[GitHub Velocity Drop] → [Support Ticket Spike] → [Cohort Churn]`

The heavy parsing happens continuously in the background, locally, incrementally. By the time the founder asks the question, the graph is already built. The cloud model just reads the map.

## SECTION 6: RAPID FIRE GLOSSARY
*Every technical term you might be tested on. One line each. Know these cold.*

### THE PRODUCT — SELFAUDIT-SPECIFIC TERMS

**Ontology** — A structured map of what something is and how its parts relate to each other. SelfAudit's ontology is the map of your business — its units, properties, and relationships — stored as local files.

**Unit** — SelfAudit's word for a defined part of a business (e.g. Customer Service, Sales, Finance). Everything inside the product is organised around units.

**Schema** — A blueprint that defines what a thing looks like — its fields, types, and rules. A unit schema defines what that unit is, what properties it tracks, and what "healthy" looks like.

**Snapshot** — A timestamped saved state. SelfAudit takes snapshots of each unit's data over time so the intelligence loop can detect trends and changes.

**Delta sync** — Only pulling what changed since the last sync, not everything. Faster, cheaper, and lighter than full data dumps.

**Intelligence loop** — The recurring cycle where SelfAudit reads your data, runs it through AI, and produces a fresh diagnosis. Runs on schedule and on trigger.

**Unit State Brief** — The compact JSON summary a Level 1 agent produces from raw unit data. It's the "cheat sheet" that gets sent to the cloud — not the raw data itself.

**Summary-Conditioned Hierarchical Inference** — SelfAudit's core architecture pattern: parse data locally, summarise it into dense briefs, send only those briefs to the cloud model for final reasoning. Keeps costs low and data private.

**Temporal Knowledge Graph** — A database of nodes (people, metrics, tools) and edges (causal relationships with timestamps). Lets the system trace second-order effects — e.g. a code slowdown in May → support spike in June → churn in July.

**Second-order effects** — Downstream consequences that aren't immediately obvious. A Sales pipeline drop today is a cash flow problem in 90 days. Catching second-order effects is the hardest and most valuable thing SelfAudit does.

**Closed loop** — A system that detects a problem, generates a response, and applies a fix — all without manual intervention. SelfAudit is moving toward a closed loop: detect anomaly → stage fix → founder approves → execute.

**Agentic execution / Actions pending** — Instead of just diagnosing, the system stages concrete actions (draft a HubSpot campaign, send a Slack alert) and waits for the founder to press 1 to execute. The action is prepared, not fired automatically.

**Concierge onboarding** — A human (currently the founder) manually configures each new account rather than making the user do it themselves. Slower to scale, but produces perfectly configured accounts and teaches what to automate later.

**Local daemon** — A background process running silently on the user's machine that watches the `.selfaudit/` folder, syncs data from integrations, and handles local operations. Like a personal assistant that never sleeps and never leaves your laptop.

**Cloud bridge / Cloudflare Worker** — A lightweight, stateless relay in the cloud that receives webhook payloads from integrations, encrypts them, and queues them for the local daemon. Holds no business data — just passes encrypted packets through.

### AI AND LLM TERMS

**LLM (Large Language Model)** — An AI model trained on massive amounts of text to understand and generate language. Claude, GPT-4, and Gemini are LLMs. SelfAudit uses Claude as its reasoning engine.

**Token** — The unit an LLM reads and writes in. Roughly 0.75 words per token. Every API call costs money per token — input tokens (what you send) and output tokens (what the model returns).

**Context window** — The maximum amount of text an LLM can hold in its "working memory" at one time. If your prompt exceeds the context window, it gets cut off. Larger context windows allow more complex reasoning.

**Hallucination** — When an LLM confidently states something that is factually wrong. Not random errors — the model generates plausible-sounding but incorrect information. Grounding with real data (like SelfAudit's local schemas) reduces hallucination significantly.

**Prompt engineering** — The art of writing instructions to an LLM to get high-quality, structured output. SelfAudit's intelligence loop is powered by carefully engineered prompts that produce diagnoses, not just summaries.

**RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** — A technique where relevant documents or data are retrieved from a database and injected into the LLM's prompt before it answers. Keeps responses grounded in real data rather than memory alone.

**Embeddings** — A way of converting text into numbers (vectors) so that similar meanings end up as similar numbers. Used to find relevant data quickly. The foundation of most AI search and RAG systems.

**Vector database** — A database optimised for storing and searching embeddings. Fast at finding "things semantically similar to this." Used in RAG pipelines to retrieve relevant context.

**Fine-tuning** — Taking a pre-trained model and training it further on your specific data to make it better at your particular task. Different from prompt engineering — it actually changes the model's weights.

**Inference** — The act of running a trained AI model to get a result. When you send a prompt to Claude and get a response, that's inference. Inference costs money per token.

**Function calling / Tool calling** — A way for an LLM to call external code (APIs, databases, scripts) rather than just generating text. SelfAudit uses this for agentic execution — the model calls the HubSpot adapter, not just writes about it.

**System prompt** — Instructions given to an LLM before the conversation starts that define how it should behave. SelfAudit's system prompt tells Claude: here is the business structure, here is what healthy looks like, here is what to produce.

**Temperature** — A setting that controls how creative or random an LLM's output is. Low temperature (0–0.3) = consistent, predictable. High temperature (0.8–1.0) = more creative, less predictable. For business diagnostics, low temperature is right.

**Grounding** — Anchoring an LLM's output to real, specific data so it can't hallucinate. SelfAudit grounds Claude with actual unit schemas, live integration data, and snapshot history before asking it to reason.

**Multi-agent system** — Multiple AI agents working together, each with a specific role. SelfAudit's Level 1 agents handle individual units; the Level 2 Chief Operating Agent handles cross-unit correlation. Divide and conquer.

**Agentic AI** — AI that doesn't just respond to questions but takes sequences of actions to complete goals. SelfAudit's execution layer is agentic — detect anomaly, draft fix, stage execution, wait for approval.

**Quantized model** — A model compressed to use less memory and compute, making it fast enough to run on a laptop. SelfAudit's Level 1 Unit Agents use small quantized models for local data processing.

**Orchestrator** — The agent or system that coordinates other agents or steps. SelfAudit's Level 2 Chief Operating Agent is the orchestrator — it takes summaries from Level 1 agents and reasons across them.

### ARCHITECTURE AND INFRASTRUCTURE

**API (Application Programming Interface)** — A defined way for two pieces of software to talk to each other. When SelfAudit connects to HubSpot, it uses HubSpot's API to request and send data.

**REST API** — The most common type of API. Uses standard web requests (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE) to read and write data. Most SaaS tools (HubSpot, Stripe, Slack) expose REST APIs.

**Webhook** — A way for one system to notify another in real time when something happens. When a deal closes in HubSpot, HubSpot sends a webhook payload to SelfAudit's cloud bridge automatically — no polling required.

**OAuth** — A secure standard for connecting apps without sharing passwords. When you click "Connect HubSpot" in SelfAudit, OAuth handles the authentication. The user authorises access; tokens are exchanged; no password is stored.

**WebSocket** — A persistent, two-way connection between a client and server — unlike a normal web request which opens, fires, and closes. SelfAudit's local daemon holds a persistent WebSocket to the message queue so it can receive payloads in real time.

**Message queue** — A holding buffer for data packets in transit. The cloud bridge drops encrypted payloads into the queue; the local daemon pulls them out. Decouples the sender and receiver so nothing is lost if one side is temporarily offline.

**JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)** — A lightweight, human-readable format for storing and transmitting structured data. Every SelfAudit file — schemas, snapshots, object files — is stored as JSON.

**Cron job** — A task scheduled to run automatically at set intervals (every hour, every night at midnight). SelfAudit's intelligence loop runs on a cron schedule — it doesn't wait for you to trigger it.

**Daemon** — A background process that runs continuously without a user interface. SelfAudit's local daemon runs silently on the machine, watching for changes and syncing data without the founder ever needing to open anything.

**Local-first** — An architecture where the primary data lives on the user's machine, not a central cloud server. Faster reads, stronger privacy, owner controls the data.

**Cloud-native** — Built to run entirely on cloud servers. Most SaaS is cloud-native. SelfAudit is deliberately not — the business ontology lives locally; only the reasoning layer touches the cloud.

**Edge computing** — Processing data as close to where it's generated as possible (on the device, not a distant server). SelfAudit's local parsing is edge computing — the founder's laptop is the edge.

**Stateless** — A system that doesn't store any data between requests. SelfAudit's Cloudflare Worker is stateless — it receives, encrypts, forwards, and forgets. No data retained.

**Encryption / Public-private key** — Scrambling data so only the intended recipient can read it. A public key encrypts; only the matching private key can decrypt. The cloud bridge encrypts payloads with the user's public key; only their local machine (with the private key) can read them.

**Serverless** — Running code without managing servers — you pay per execution, not per hour. Cloudflare Workers are serverless. Cost is near-zero at low volume.

**Binary / Executable** — A compiled program that runs directly on an operating system without needing an interpreter. "Download the binary" means download the SelfAudit desktop app and run it directly.

**Latency** — The delay between an action and its response. Low latency = fast. Local reads have near-zero latency because nothing leaves the machine.

**Throughput** — How much data a system can process per unit of time. High throughput = handles lots of requests simultaneously.

**Scalability** — A system's ability to handle more load without breaking. Horizontal scaling = add more machines. Vertical scaling = make the existing machine bigger.

**Microservices** — Breaking an application into small, independent services that each do one thing. Opposite of a monolith. Each SelfAudit unit agent is microservice-like — isolated, single-purpose.

**Monolith** — A single large application where everything runs together. Simpler to build early, harder to scale later.

**Cache / Caching** — Storing a computed result so you don't have to compute it again. SelfAudit's snapshot files are effectively a cache of the unit's last known state.

**CDN (Content Delivery Network)** — A network of servers around the world that serves content from the location closest to the user. Cloudflare operates a CDN. Reduces latency for global users.

**RBAC (Role-Based Access Control)** — Restricting system access based on a user's role. SelfAudit implements RBAC at the file-system level — a Sales Manager's session token can't decrypt the finance folder.

**CI/CD (Continuous Integration / Continuous Deployment)** — Automating the process of testing and deploying code changes. Every time a developer pushes code, CI/CD runs tests and deploys automatically if they pass.

**Git / Version control** — A system for tracking changes to code over time. SelfAudit's entire local ontology is version-controllable because it's just files — `git commit` your business model.

### WEB AND FRONTEND

**JavaScript** — The programming language of the web. Runs in browsers and (via Node.js) on servers. SelfAudit's frontend is built with it.

**TypeScript** — JavaScript with types added — catches errors before they happen. Stricter, more predictable for large codebases.

**Next.js** — A React framework for building web applications. Handles routing, server-side rendering, and deployment. SelfAudit's frontend runs on Next.js.

**Node.js** — JavaScript running on a server (not a browser). SelfAudit's backend API is Node.js.

**React** — A JavaScript library for building user interfaces. Next.js is built on React.

**Route / Routing** — Defining what happens when a user visits a specific URL. `/api/units` might be the route that returns unit data. The route is the address; the handler is what happens there.

**Middleware** — Code that runs between a request arriving and a response being sent. Used for authentication checks, logging, rate limiting — the checkpoint before you get to the destination.

**Gateway / API gateway** — A single entry point that routes API requests to the right internal service. Acts as a traffic controller and security layer between the outside world and your backend.

**Frontend** — What the user sees and interacts with. The browser-facing layer — buttons, screens, layouts.

**Backend** — The server-side logic — databases, business rules, API endpoints. The user never sees it directly.

**Environment variables** — Configuration values stored outside the code (API keys, database URLs). Never hardcoded in the source — kept secret, loaded at runtime.

**SDK (Software Development Kit)** — A packaged set of tools for building with a specific platform. Anthropic has an SDK for calling Claude. Using the SDK is easier than writing raw API calls.

**CLI (Command Line Interface)** — A text-based interface for interacting with software via a terminal. `selfaudit init` would be a CLI command.

### DATABASES AND DATA

**Database** — Organised storage for data with the ability to query it. SelfAudit uses the local file system as its primary database — unconventional but intentional.

**SQL** — Structured Query Language. The standard language for querying relational databases. `SELECT * FROM units WHERE status = 'at_risk'` is SQL.

**Relational database** — Data stored in tables with rows and columns, linked by relationships. Postgres, MySQL. Traditional, mature, reliable.

**Graph database** — Data stored as nodes and edges (relationships). Better than relational databases for questions like "what caused what." SelfAudit's Temporal Knowledge Graph is conceptually a graph database.

**Time-series data** — Data points indexed by time. Revenue at 9am, tickets at 10am, churn rate at end of day. SelfAudit's snapshots are time-series data — tracking how each unit's state changes over time.

**ETL (Extract, Transform, Load)** — The process of pulling data from a source, cleaning/restructuring it, and loading it into a destination. SelfAudit's data adapters are doing ETL — pulling from HubSpot, transforming to schema format, writing to local files.

### BUSINESS AND PRODUCT TERMS

**SaaS (Software as a Service)** — Software delivered over the internet on a subscription basis. SelfAudit is SaaS — $999/month, no installation required beyond the local binary.

**ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)** — Total subscription revenue expected over a year. 100 customers × $999/month × 12 = $1.2M ARR.

**MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)** — Monthly version. 100 customers × $999 = $99,900 MRR.

**Churn** — The rate at which customers cancel. 5% monthly churn means 5% of your customers leave each month.

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** — What it costs to acquire one customer. SelfAudit's CAC is near-zero early on — direct outreach, no paid ads.

**LTV (Lifetime Value)** — Total revenue expected from a customer over their lifetime. LTV:CAC ratio is a key health metric.

**COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)** — The direct costs of delivering the product. For SelfAudit: Claude API costs + Twilio + Cloudflare. Target: ~$38–50/month per user.

**Gross margin** — Revenue minus COGS, expressed as a percentage. SelfAudit's gross margin is ~95% — the structural advantage of local-first architecture.

**B2B** — Business-to-business. Selling to companies, not individuals. SelfAudit is B2B — the buyer is a founder or COO.

**ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — The specific type of customer who gets the most value, converts easiest, and stays longest. SelfAudit's ICP: $2M–$10M ARR, non-technical Founder Mode operator, 15–50 employees.

**GTM (Go-to-Market)** — The strategy for how you reach and acquire customers. SelfAudit's GTM: direct outreach → demo → concierge onboarding → word of mouth.

**PLG (Product-Led Growth)** — Growth driven by the product itself (free trial, viral loops, self-serve). Contrasts with sales-led growth. SelfAudit is initially sales-led (every buyer gets a demo call).

**Wedge strategy** — Entering a large market through a narrow, specific beachhead segment first. SelfAudit's wedge: $2–10M ARR non-technical operators. Expand from there.

**PMF (Product-Market Fit)** — When a product genuinely solves a problem that a specific market desperately has. Signalled by retention, word of mouth, and users being upset at the thought of losing the product.

**Second-order effects** — Consequences that happen downstream from an initial cause, not immediately obvious. A YC-level question about your business — always be ready to show you've thought two steps ahead.

**Closed loop (business)** — A system where output feeds back as input — detect, act, measure, adjust, repeat. SelfAudit aims to be a closed loop for business operations.
