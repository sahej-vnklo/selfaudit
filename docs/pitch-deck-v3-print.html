The Gap
33 million SMBs in the US alone. Zero cross-functional visibility.

Every business generates
second-order effects.
None of them can see them.

A decision made in one area of a business creates consequences in a completely different area — weeks or months later. The delay between the two is long enough that the connection disappears.

By the time the damage arrives, the decision that caused it is already forgotten. Three more decisions have been made on top of it. All of them pointed at the symptom, not the cause.

Sales watches pipeline. Finance watches burn. Operations watches throughput. Nobody is watching the chain running between them. That gap is where most business value is destroyed.

A finance decision creates pressure in ops. Ops degrades customer experience. Retention drops. Revenue flatlines ninety days later — and gets diagnosed as a sales problem.

The Sales Hiring Trap

Revenue is flat. Founder hires three salespeople. Six months later revenue is still flat. The salespeople did everything right. The problem was retention — customers churning in 60 days, offsetting every new deal.

Finance diagnosed as sales → wrong function hired → retention problem grew 6 more months → cost: 2× salary + problem still unsolved.

The Support Cut

Cash pressure. Support team reduced to extend runway. Savings: immediate. What takes 90 days to arrive: churn increase, review damage, referral slowdown, higher CAC on every future acquisition.

Finance cut ops → ops degraded customer experience → churn rose → CAC rose → financial position worse than before the cut.

The Price Increase

Margin pressure. Price raised. Sales slows. CEO reads it as execution failure and replaces the sales lead. New leader inherits a structural problem no execution change can fix. Margin problem still unsolved. Disrupted team on top.

Finance decision → sales velocity dropped → misdiagnosed → talent fired → disruption added → original problem persisted.

A $1M business with one broken cross-functional chain loses an estimated $230K per year — absorbed as friction, rework, and wrong decisions. It does not show on the P&L. It shows in the ceiling the business cannot break through.
