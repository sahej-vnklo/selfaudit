# How To Talk About It — SelfAudit Talk Track

*This is the core conversation sequence. Use it for LinkedIn posts, investor pitches, cold outreach, landing page copy, or any conversation where you are introducing the problem. The structure is always the same: situation → diagnosis → concept → solution earns its place.*

---

## The Sequence

---

**Start with a situation they have lived.**

You hired to fix revenue.
Six months later, revenue is still flat.
The salespeople did everything right.

Sound familiar?

---

**Let it sit. Then name what actually happened.**

The problem was never sales.

It was retention. Customers were churning in sixty days — quietly, consistently — offsetting every new deal your team closed.

The data was there. It was just sitting in a different column, watched by a different person, on a different dashboard.

Nobody was watching both at the same time.

So you fixed the symptom. The cause kept running.

---

**Then the follow-on hits.**

And here is the part that actually costs you.

By the time you saw it — six months later, in a revenue number that still would not move — you had already made three more decisions on top of the first wrong one.

You adjusted the sales process. You changed the compensation structure. You considered replacing the sales lead.

All of it pointed at the symptom.

None of it touched the cause.

---

**Name it. One sentence.**

That is second-order effects.

A decision made in one part of your business creates a consequence in a completely different part — and the delay between the two is long enough that the connection disappears.

By the time the damage arrives, the decision that caused it is six months old and already forgotten.

---

**Widen it. Make them feel it in their own business.**

It is not just a sales story.

You cut support to save cash. Churn rises ninety days later. Looks like a retention problem.

You raise prices to fix margins. Sales slows down. Looks like an execution problem.

You land a big enterprise client. Your roadmap shifts toward their needs. Your core customers quietly start leaving. Looks like a product problem.

Every time — the decision was made in one place. The damage landed somewhere else. And the gap between the two was wide enough that nobody connected them until it was too late to connect them cheaply.

---

**The question that makes them self-diagnose.**

Think about the last time something broke in your business.

Where did you find the problem?

Now go back further. What decision, made three to six months earlier, in a completely different part of the operation, started the chain?

Most founders cannot answer that question. Not because they are not smart enough. Because they have never had a system that watches across all of it at once.

---

**Now the product earns its place.**

That is the only thing SelfAudit is built to do.

Not reports. Not dashboards. Not another tool that shows you what already happened in the function you were already watching.

A system that watches the relationship between all of them — continuously — and tells you when a decision made today is forming a chain that will land somewhere else in ninety days.

The hiring-sales-to-fix-retention chain. The support-cut-to-churn chain. The price-increase-to-sales-misdiagnosis chain.

SelfAudit is designed to name those chains while they are one link long.

Not after the damage is done.
Not after you have already made three more wrong decisions on top of the first one.
While it is still a conversation, not a crisis.

## Variations — Short Version + One-Liner Per Audience

---

### 2. The Founder Burning Cash Without Knowing Why

You are watching the burn rate and it does not add up.

Revenue is there. The team is not unusually large. Nothing looks obviously wrong in any single area. But the number keeps moving in the wrong direction and nobody can point to exactly why.

That is usually not one problem. It is four small inefficiencies running simultaneously across different parts of the operation — each one invisible from inside the function it lives in, collectively expensive enough to matter.

Second-order effects do not just damage revenue. They quietly inflate cost. A broken handoff generates rework. Rework consumes time. Time consumed on rework is time not spent on growth. The burn rate reflects all of it and explains none of it.

SelfAudit watches where the inefficiencies are compounding across functions — before they become the number you cannot explain.

**One-liner:** Your burn rate is not one problem. It is several small ones running in different parts of your business at the same time. SelfAudit finds where they connect.

---

### 3. The Ops Person Firefighting the Same Problems Every Week

The same escalation. Different week.

You fixed it last time. You fixed it the time before. It keeps coming back in a slightly different form and you are starting to wonder if the operation is just broken in a way that cannot be fixed.

It is not broken. It is undiagnosed.

The escalation you keep fixing is a symptom. The cause is upstream — a process gap, a decision bottleneck, a handoff that has never been clearly owned — and it keeps generating the same downstream fire because nobody has ever traced the chain from the fire back to the source.

Fixing symptoms is expensive. Fixing the source is a one-time conversation.

SelfAudit traces the chain from the recurring problem back to where it actually starts.

**One-liner:** If the same problem keeps coming back, you are fixing the symptom. SelfAudit finds where the chain actually starts.

---

### 4. The Investor or Board Member

You have seen this before.

A portfolio company hits a wall. The leadership team is working hard, the market is there, the product works. But something is compounding inside the operation that the metrics are not surfacing — and by the time it shows up in the numbers you are watching, it has already been running for two quarters.

Nokia's engineers knew. The information just never made it to the people who could act on it. WeWork's structure was visible in the filings. But nobody was watching the relationship between the obligations and the revenue in real time.

The companies that avoid this are not smarter. They have better visibility into what is running between functions — not inside them.

SelfAudit is that visibility layer for businesses that have never had access to it.

**One-liner:** The companies in your portfolio that hit unexpected walls are not making bad decisions. They are making decisions without seeing the second-order effects forming across functions. SelfAudit is the early warning system they have never had.

### 5. The Founder Who Just Fired Someone and the Problem Is Still There

You made the call. It was not easy. You were certain it was the right move.

The problem is still there.

That is the clearest possible signal that the problem was never the person. It was the system the person was operating inside — the process that was unclear, the decision authority that was undefined, the upstream input that was always broken before it reached them.

People do not create second-order effects. Broken systems do. People just become visible when the effects land.

SelfAudit diagnoses the system, not the person — so the next hire does not inherit the same broken chain.

**One-liner:** If the problem survived the person you let go, the problem was never the person. SelfAudit finds what in the system is actually generating it.
