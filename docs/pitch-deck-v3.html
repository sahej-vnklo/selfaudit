The Concept
A term every serious investor knows

Second-order effects.

First-order thinking sees the immediate result of a decision. Second-order thinking asks what happens next — and in which other part of the business that lands.

Most businesses only think first-order. Not because their leaders are not smart. Because they have no system that shows them the second order forming in real time.

They see sales numbers. Burn rate. Churn. Each metric in its own column, watched by its own team. What they do not see is the chain running between them.

A decision made in finance creates pressure in operations. That pressure drops retention. That drop shows up in revenue — ninety days later, looking like a sales problem.

The Sales Hiring Trap

Revenue is flat. Founder hires three salespeople. Six months later revenue is still flat. The salespeople did everything right. The problem was retention — customers churning in 60 days, offsetting every new deal closed.

Finance diagnosed as sales → wrong function hired → retention problem grew 6 months longer → total cost: 2× salary + the original problem unsolved.

The Support Cut

Cash pressure. Support team reduced to extend runway. Savings: immediate and real. What takes 90 days to arrive: churn increase, review damage, referral slowdown, higher cost of every future acquisition.

Finance cut ops → ops degraded customer experience → churn rose → CAC rose → financial position worse than before the cut.

The Price Increase

Margin pressure. Price raised. Sales slows. CEO reads it as execution failure and replaces the sales lead. New leader inherits a structural problem no execution change can fix. Margin problem still unsolved. Disrupted sales team added on top.

Finance decision → sales velocity dropped → misdiagnosed → talent fired → disruption added → original problem persisted.

Every chain has the same anatomy: a decision in one area, a consequence in another, a delay long enough that the connection disappears.
